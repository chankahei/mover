01 / system architecture

Event-driven decisioning loop

The system should not generate one article per event. It should generate many article candidates per event, each representing a different causal hypothesis about framing, timing, audience, and CTA.

Architecture flow

Signal ingestion to production feedback loop.

Mermaid 
flowchart TD
  A[External & internal signals] --> B[Event bus]
  B --> C[Entity resolution]
  C --> D[Real-time feature store]
  D --> E[User clustering]
  D --> F[Portfolio exposure engine]
  E --> G[Causal graph inference]
  F --> G
  G --> H[Article candidate generator]
  H --> I[Content assembly]
  G --> J[Ranking, timing & pacing]
  I --> K[Delivery channels]
  J --> K
  K --> L[Outcome tracking]
  L --> M[Attribution & graph update]
  M -. weekly feedback .-> G
A

Normalize and resolve

Map news, prices, filings, social attention, portfolio events, and house views into canonical tickers, sectors, themes, and time buckets.

B

Generate hypotheses

Create article candidates for the same event across urgency, opportunity, risk, peer comparison, portfolio impact, social proof, and house-view frames.

C

Rank by incremental lift

Select candidates using expected incremental CTR, expected trade lift, urgency, relevance, freshness, confidence, and inventory fit.

D

Learn from outcomes

Use exposure logs, clicks, CTA actions, order events, trades, and holdouts to update graph confidence and decision policy.

02 / causal inference

Day-one causal graph, weekly refutation

The causal graph exists from the first release. What changes over time is not the architecture, but the amount of observable context available to propose, refute, and promote graph relationships.

Discovery pipeline

Candidate edges are proposed from lagged time-series models and SHAP values.

weekly 
flowchart TD
  A[Normalize all data into time series] --> B[Create lagged features]
  B --> C[Train model set per target variable]
  C --> D[Compute SHAP importance]
  D --> E[Propose candidate graph edges]
  E --> F[Run refutation pipeline]
  F --> G{Survives tests?}
  G -- Yes --> H[Promote to production graph]
  G -- No --> I[Reject or keep low confidence]
  H --> J[Article policy service]
  I --> K[Candidate backlog]
  J --> L[Weekly rebuild]
  K --> L
  L --> A

1. Time-series substrate

All signals are timestamped and aligned to decisioning grains such as one minute, five minutes, hourly, daily, or event-relative time.

2. Lagged prediction

Each target is predicted with other time-series variables shifted by small lags to prevent same-timestamp leakage and preserve temporal plausibility.

3. SHAP edge proposals

Feature importance identifies candidate graph edges. These are hypotheses, not proof.

4. Refute before production

Temporal holdouts, placebo tests, delayed-treatment tests, negative controls, confounder checks, and cluster stability tests decide what survives.

03 / signal catalog

Signals as triggers, features, and graph nodes

Each signal can be used to trigger an article, rank an article, personalize content, or become a node in the causal graph. The strongest product emerges when public urgency is fused with private portfolio and behavior context.

Market

Price, volume, volatility

Intraday move, open gap, 52-week high/low, relative strength, volume spike, volatility spike, options volume, ETF flows.

Primary use: urgency and timing.
Corporate

Earnings and disclosures

Earnings surprise, guidance, buyback, dividend change, M&A, management change, regulatory decision, insider activity, filings.

Primary use: direct security-specific catalysts.
News

Media and narrative shifts

Breaking news, sentiment shift, coverage spike, CEO interview, competitor news, supply chain headlines, geopolitical events.

Primary use: story generation and novelty.
Macro

Cross-asset context

CPI, rates, yield curve, oil, gold, FX, jobs, PMI, country policy and central bank announcements.

Primary use: portfolio exposure mapping.
Portfolio

Holdings and exposure

Direct holding, watchlist exposure, sector concentration, unrealized gain/loss, position size, cash band, recent buy/sell.

Primary use: personal relevance.
Behavior

Intent and funnel state

Article clicks, dwell time, asset page views, search queries, watchlist actions, alert creation, order ticket opens, abandoned orders.

Primary use: trade readiness.
Social

Viral attention

Twitter/X mentions, Reddit spikes, creator coverage, search trend spikes, forum discussion, influencer commentary, meme velocity.

Primary use: curiosity and FOMO intensity.
Network

Cohort and friend momentum

Portfolio-similar trading spikes, cohort watchlist activity, friend-circle topic trends, group chat momentum, theme popularity.

Primary use: social proof and conversation utility.
House view

Proprietary conviction

Internal analyst notes, view changes, valuation gaps, sentiment models, editorial priority, theme baskets, conversion propensity.

Primary use: differentiated article substance.

04 / roadmap

Data availability unlocks better goals

The system stays broadly the same. Each new data layer improves article quality, CTA quality, timing quality, and the system’s ability to create new user behavior.

Data availability ladder

From observable basics to longitudinal behavior shaping.

roadmap 
flowchart LR
  S0[Measurement] --> S1[Public market data]
  S1 --> S2[Watchlist & preferences]
  S2 --> S3[Brokerage portfolio]
  S3 --> S4[Behavioral intent]
  S4 --> S5[Experimentation]
  S5 --> S6[Social & network]
  S6 --> S7[House view]
  S7 --> S8[Longitudinal journey]
Stage 0

Measurement availability

Make the graph observable through exposure logs, outcome events, article metadata, and holdouts.

Behavior unlocked: baseline article consumption.
Stage 1

Public market data

Trigger timely market-aware articles using price, volume, earnings, corporate actions, news, and macro data.

Behavior unlocked: urgency-driven clicks.
Stage 2

Watchlist and preference

Move from market relevance to user-interest relevance using watchlists, search, followed tickers, and topic clicks.

Behavior unlocked: repeat clicks and watchlist actions.
Stage 3

Brokerage portfolio

Convert generic events into personal portfolio events through holdings, exposure, recent trades, and cash readiness.

Behavior unlocked: higher click-to-trade conversion.
Stage 4

Behavioral intent

Identify users close to action using asset views, chart interactions, alerts, order tickets, and abandoned orders.

Behavior unlocked: more order ticket opens.
Stage 5

Experimentation

Separate raw propensity from incremental lift using treatments, delayed sends, negative controls, and refutation.

Behavior unlocked: better trade lift per impression.
Stage 6

Social and network

Add peer momentum and social proof through cohort activity, portfolio-similar users, and friend-circle themes.

Behavior unlocked: curiosity and discussion-driven engagement.
Stage 7

House view

Improve article substance with proprietary research, view changes, valuation gaps, conviction, and theme baskets.

Behavior unlocked: research-led trading.
Stage 8

Longitudinal journey

Optimize sequences across articles, alerts, asset views, watchlists, and order moments.

Behavior unlocked: app-as-daily-trading-prompt.

05 / build scope

MVP with graph priors from the start

The MVP should include causal graph inference on day one, but the graph is sparse and uncertainty-aware. It combines structural priors, deterministic relevance rules, and early outcome learning.

Core product

  • Public event ingestion for price moves, news, earnings, and corporate actions.
  • Portfolio and watchlist matching.
  • Article candidate generation from templates.
  • Basic personalization inserts and CTA variants.

Decisioning

  • Day-one causal graph with structural priors.
  • Relevance, urgency, freshness, confidence, and fatigue scoring.
  • Push and in-app delivery orchestration.
  • Manual analyst console for top triggers and suppressions.

Learning loop

  • Exposure and outcome logging.
  • CTR, order ticket open, and executed trade attribution.
  • Holdout groups and delayed-send controls.
  • Weekly graph discovery, refutation, promotion, and rollback.
